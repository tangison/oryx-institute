    13→    13→## 1. Purpose and authority
    14→    14→
    15→    15→This file is the master design contract for Oryx Institute. It defines how the institution should look, feel and behave across websites, learner systems, company profiles, prospectuses, forms, certificates, presentations, signage, campaigns and physical spaces.
    16→    16→
    17→    17→`DESIGN.md` is a project-owned specification, not a universal file standard. Its rules are structured so that designers, developers, document producers and AI agents can apply the same decisions without inventing new styles.

    69→    69→## 3. Design thesis
    70→    70→
    71→    71→Oryx Institute is **Namibian institutional modernism**.
    72→    72→
    73→    73→The visual system combines:
    74→    74→
    75→    75→- the authority of a serious academic institution;
    76→    76→- the clarity of a modern vocational service;
    77→    77→- the restraint and material warmth of Namibia;
    78→    78→- the confidence of a new institution that does not need visual noise to appear credible.
    79→    79→
    80→    80→The governing visual idea is **the path cut through the landscape**. It appears through disciplined diagonal lines, cropped architectural forms, paths, thresholds, light and negative space. The diagonal is derived from the supplied shield, but the shield itself must never be reconstructed from layout elements.
    81→    81→
    82→    82→The work should feel commissioned for Oryx. It must not look like a generic university template, an AI start-up, a bank, a government form or a collection of fashionable cards.
    83→    83→
    84→    84→## 4. Design principles
    85→    85→
    86→    86→### 4.1 Quiet authority
    87→    87→
    88→    88→Use scale, proportion, typography and space before decoration. A page may be visually powerful while remaining calm.
    89→    89→
    90→    90→### 4.2 Namibia without cliché
    91→    91→
    92→    92→Use Namibian landscape, light, material, architecture and working life with dignity. Avoid flags as decoration, tourist stereotypes, wildlife collages and ornamental patterns unrelated to the institution.
    93→    93→
    94→    94→### 4.3 Evidence over spectacle
    95→    95→
    96→    96→Qualifications, outcomes, processes and claims must be precise. Design should help users understand evidence, not conceal weak information behind effects.
    97→    97→
    98→    98→### 4.4 Familiar core, modern delivery
    99→    99→
   100→   100→The interface may feel modern, but programmes must remain recognisable to learners, families, employers and regulators.
   101→   101→
   102→   102→### 4.5 One strong idea per composition
   103→   103→
   104→   104→Each page, spread, section or screen should have one dominant message and one dominant visual subject.
   105→   105→
   106→   106→### 4.6 Restraint is not emptiness
   107→   107→
   108→   108→Negative space must strengthen hierarchy. It must not be used to hide missing content or create unnecessarily long pages.
   109→   109→
   110→   110→### 4.7 Accessible by default
   111→   111→
   112→   112→Colour, type, motion, focus and component behaviour must meet the accessibility rules in this document from the first design, not as a correction at the end.

   114→   114→## 5. Logo system
   115→   115→
   116→   116→### 5.1 Authoritative assets
   117→   117→
   118→   118→Use only the supplied Oryx Institute assets:
   119→   119→
   120→   120→- primary horizontal lock-up;
   121→   121→- approved stacked lock-up;
   122→   122→- shield or symbol-only mark;
   123→   123→- approved reversed variations.
   124→   124→
   125→   125→Never:
   126→   126→
   127→   127→- redraw or regenerate the mark;
   128→   128→- type the wordmark manually;
   129→   129→- replace the Greek text;
   130→   130→- change proportions;
   131→   131→- rotate, skew, outline or add effects;
   132→   132→- add a watermark to the logo;
   133→   133→- allow image-generation tools to recreate it;
   134→   134→- place text or another symbol inside the shield;
   135→   135→- place the maroon logo on a maroon field.
   136→   136→
   137→   137→Generated images must contain no logo. Add the authentic logo after image generation using deterministic layout software.
   138→   138→
   139→   139→### 5.2 Logo-background pairings
   140→   140→
   141→   141→| Background | Approved logo treatment |
   142→   142→|---|---|
   143→   143→| Cream or white | Black and maroon primary lock-up |
   144→   144→| Very light photography | Black and maroon only if contrast and clear space remain strong |
   145→   145→| Primary maroon | Cream or white reversed logo |
   146→   146→| Dark maroon | Cream or white reversed logo |
   147→   147→| Black | Cream or white reversed logo |
   148→   148→| Dark or detailed photography | Reversed logo on a controlled dark overlay or clear field |
   149→   149→
   150→   150→Do not place the primary maroon shield directly on primary maroon, dark maroon or visually similar photography.
   151→   151→
   152→   152→### 5.3 Clear space
   153→   153→
   154→   154→Use the shield width as the base unit **S**.
   155→   155→
   156→   156→- Minimum clear space around a full lock-up: `0.35S`.
   157→   157→- Preferred clear space: `0.5S`.
   158→   158→- Keep all type, trim lines, folds and image edges outside the minimum clear space.
   159→   159→
   160→   160→Where an original vector guide supplies a different measured exclusion zone, the original vector guide takes precedence.
   161→   161→
   162→   162→### 5.4 Minimum size
   163→   163→
   164→   164→- Digital full lock-up: 144 CSS px wide minimum.
   165→   165→- Digital shield: 28 CSS px wide minimum.
   166→   166→- Print full lock-up: 35 mm wide minimum.
   167→   167→- Print shield: 9 mm wide minimum.
   168→   168→
   169→   169→Below these sizes, use the shield only or an approved simplified lock-up. Never simplify the artwork manually.
   170→   170→
   171→   171→### 5.5 Repetition
   172→   172→
   173→   173→The logo should anchor a composition, not wallpaper it.
   174→   174→
   175→   175→- Website: once in the navigation and once in the footer is normally enough.
   176→   176→- Editorial spread: once per spread unless a legal or document-control requirement justifies another use.
   177→   177→- Forms and institutional templates: logo in the header; a faint symbol may be used as a controlled watermark where necessary.
   178→   178→- Social artwork: one logo only.

   246→   246→## 7. Typography
   247→   247→
   248→   248→### 7.1 Font hierarchy
   249→   249→
   250→   250→#### Display
   251→   251→
   252→   252→**First choice:** Trajan Pro 3 or Trajan Pro, properly licensed through Adobe Fonts or another valid licence.
   253→   253→
   254→   254→Use for:
   255→   255→
   256→   256→- major institutional titles;
   257→   257→- cover titles;
   258→   258→- short section openers;
   259→   259→- ceremonial material;
   260→   260→- selected navigation or labels where all-capitals remain readable.
   261→   261→
   262→   262→Do not use Trajan for:
   263→   263→
   264→   264→- body paragraphs;
   265→   265→- long quotations;
   266→   266→- form labels and inputs;
   267→   267→- dense tables;
   268→   268→- small mobile text;
   269→   269→- sentence-case interface messages.
   270→   270→
   271→   271→Trajan is primarily an all-capitals display face. Do not force it into roles requiring a complete lowercase reading experience.
   272→   272→
   273→   273→#### Open fallback display
   274→   274→
   275→   275→**Cinzel** is the approved open-source substitute when Trajan is unavailable or cannot legally be embedded.
   276→   276→
   277→   277→Cinzel must be treated as a fallback, not mixed with Trajan in one product or publication.
   278→   278→
   279→   279→#### Text and interface
   280→   280→
   281→   281→**Source Sans 3** is the primary body and interface family.
   282→   282→
   283→   283→Use it for:
   284→   284→
   285→   285→- body copy;
   286→   286→- forms;
   287→   287→- buttons;
   288→   288→- labels;
   289→   289→- navigation support text;
   290→   290→- tables;
   291→   291→- captions;
   292→   292→- programme information;
   293→   293→- data and dashboards.
   294→   294→
   295→   295→### 7.2 Font stacks
   296→   296→
   297→   297→```css
   298→   298→:root {
   299→   299→  --font-display-licensed:
   300→   300→    "Trajan Pro 3",
   301→   301→    "Trajan Pro",
   302→   302→    "Cinzel",
   303→   303→    "Times New Roman",
   304→   304→    serif;
   305→   305→
   306→   306→  --font-display-open:
   307→   307→    "Cinzel",
   308→   308→    "Times New Roman",
   309→   309→    Georgia,
   310→   310→    serif;
   311→   311→
   312→   312→  --font-body:
   313→   313→    "Source Sans 3",
   314→   314→    "Source Sans Pro",
   315→   315→    "Segoe UI",
   316→   316→    Arial,
   317→   317→    sans-serif;
   318→   318→}
   319→   319→```
   320→   320→
   321→   321→Implementation rule:
   322→   322→
   323→   323→- Use `--font-display-licensed` only when Trajan is available under a valid licence.
   324→   324→- Use `--font-display-open` in public builds that cannot load Trajan legally.
   325→   325→- Never download an unverified Trajan file from a third-party font website.
   326→   326→- Self-host Source Sans 3 in WOFF2 where practical, or load it from a trusted provider.
   327→   327→
   328→   328→### 7.3 Type scale
   329→   329→
   330→   330→Use `rem` for digital type. The root remains `16px` unless a user preference overrides it.
   331→   331→
   332→   332→| Token | Desktop | Mobile | Line height | Use |
   333→   333→|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
   334→   334→| `type.display-xl` | 4.5rem | 2.75rem | 0.98 | Large campaign or home hero |
   335→   335→| `type.display-lg` | 3.5rem | 2.25rem | 1.02 | Major page title |
   336→   336→| `type.h1` | 2.75rem | 2rem | 1.08 | Standard page heading |
   337→   337→| `type.h2` | 2rem | 1.625rem | 1.16 | Major section |
   338→   338→| `type.h3` | 1.5rem | 1.375rem | 1.25 | Subsection |
   339→   339→| `type.h4` | 1.25rem | 1.125rem | 1.3 | Component heading |
   340→   340→| `type.body-lg` | 1.25rem | 1.125rem | 1.55 | Introductory copy |
   341→   341→| `type.body` | 1.0625rem | 1rem | 1.6 | Primary reading text |
   342→   342→| `type.body-sm` | 0.9375rem | 0.9375rem | 1.5 | Supporting copy |
   343→   343→| `type.caption` | 0.8125rem | 0.8125rem | 1.45 | Caption and metadata |
   344→   344→
   345→   345→Do not set essential digital text below `0.8125rem`. Forms and long-form text should normally remain at least `1rem`.
   346→   346→
   347→   347→### 7.4 Tracking and casing
   348→   348→
   349→   349→- Display titles: `0.04em` to `0.12em`, depending on length.
   350→   350→- Short all-capital labels: `0.10em` to `0.18em`.
   351→   351→- Source Sans 3 body copy: normal tracking.
   352→   352→- Never use all capitals for paragraphs, instructions or error messages.
   353→   353→- Avoid artificial small capitals if the font does not contain real small-cap glyphs.
   354→   354→
   355→   355→### 7.5 Text measure and alignment
   356→   356→
   357→   357→- Ideal body line length: 55 to 75 characters.
   358→   358→- Maximum long-form measure: `72ch`.
   359→   359→- Body copy is left-aligned on digital screens.
   360→   360→- Fully justified type is reserved for carefully typeset print editorial work and must not create visible rivers.
   361→   361→- Headings may be centred only for ceremonial, cover or short landing-page moments.
   362→   362→
   363→   363→## 8. Grid and layout
   364→   364→
   365→   365→### 8.1 Digital container
   366→   366→
   367→   367→```css
   368→   368→--container-max: 90rem;       /* 1440px */
   369→   369→--container-reading: 72ch;
   370→   370→--page-gutter-mobile: 1.25rem;
   371→   371→--page-gutter-tablet: 2rem;
   372→   372→--page-gutter-desktop: 4rem;
   373→   373→```
   374→   374→
   375→   375→The site may use the full viewport for photography, but readable content remains constrained.
   376→   376→
   377→   377→### 8.2 Grid
   378→   378→
   379→   379→| Viewport | Columns | Gutter | Outer margin |
   380→   380→|---|---:|---:|---:|
   381→   381→| Mobile, below 640px | 4 | 16px | 20px |
   382→   382→| Tablet, 640px to 1023px | 8 | 24px | 32px |
   383→   383→| Desktop, 1024px and above | 12 | 24px to 32px | 48px to 64px |
   384→   384→
   385→   385→Start with one column on small screens. Introduce complexity only when content requires it.
   386→   386→
   387→   387→### 8.3 Spacing scale
   388→   388→
   389→   389→Use a consistent 4px base with an 8px working rhythm.
   390→   390→
   391→   391→```css
   392→   392→--space-0: 0;
   393→   393→--space-1: 0.25rem;  /* 4 */
   394→   394→--space-2: 0.5rem;   /* 8 */
   395→   395→--space-3: 0.75rem;  /* 12 */
   396→   396→--space-4: 1rem;     /* 16 */
   397→   397→--space-5: 1.5rem;   /* 24 */
   398→   398→--space-6: 2rem;     /* 32 */
   399→   399→--space-7: 3rem;     /* 48 */
   400→   400→--space-8: 4rem;     /* 64 */
   401→   401→--space-9: 6rem;     /* 96 */
   402→   402→--space-10: 8rem;    /* 128 */
   403→   403→```
   404→   404→
   405→   405→Large digital sections may use `clamp()` between `--space-8` and `--space-10`. Do not introduce arbitrary one-off spacing without documenting the need.
   406→   406→
   407→   407→### 8.4 Composition
   408→   408→
   409→   409→- Prefer asymmetric editorial balance over perfect centring.
   410→   410→- Use large quiet fields beside one concentrated image or text block.
   411→   411→- Align elements to common axes.
   412→   412→- Let one image cross a grid boundary only when it strengthens the main idea.
   413→   413→- Do not repeat identical three-card or four-card rows down a page.
   414→   414→- Do not use decorative sidebars that contain no meaningful information.
   415→   415→
   416→   416→## 9. Shape, border and depth
   417→   417→
   418→   418→### 9.1 Shape language
   419→   419→
   420→   420→Oryx is primarily rectilinear.
   421→   421→
   422→   422→- Default corner radius: `0`.
   423→   423→- Small functional radius: `2px`.
   424→   424→- Maximum normal radius: `4px`.
   425→   425→- Circular shapes are reserved for portraits, status dots and genuinely circular data.
   426→   426→- Pill shapes are reserved for short status labels only.
   427→   427→
   428→   428→### 9.2 Diagonal motif
   429→   429→
   430→   430→The diagonal motif comes from the authentic shield.
   431→   431→
   432→   432→- Sample the angle from the original vector asset.
   433→   433→- Use it as a crop, rule, reveal, image edge or transition.
   434→   434→- Use one strong diagonal event per composition.
   435→   435→- Never assemble diagonals into an imitation of the shield.
   436→   436→- Never use multiple random slashes as decoration.
   437→   437→
   438→   438→### 9.3 Borders
   439→   439→
   440→   440→```css
   441→   441→--border-subtle: 1px solid #E9E1D8;
   442→   442→--border-default: 1px solid #CFC6BD;
   443→   443→--border-strong: 2px solid #171717;
   444→   444→--border-accent: 2px solid #7A0F1E;
   445→   445→```
   446→   446→
   447→   447→### 9.4 Shadows
   448→   448→
   449→   449→Shadows should be nearly invisible.
   450→   450→
   451→   451→```css
   452→   452→--shadow-low: 0 1px 2px rgba(23, 23, 23, 0.08);
   453→   453→--shadow-raised: 0 12px 30px rgba(23, 23, 23, 0.10);
   454→   454→```
   455→   455→
   456→   456→Do not use glowing shadows, deep floating cards or glassmorphism.
   457→   457→
   458→   458→## 10. Imagery
   459→   459→
   460→   460→### 10.1 Image families
   461→   461→
   462→   462→Build a broad image library so the same image is not repeated across unrelated sections.

   466→   466→   - mountains and dry river landscapes;
   467→   467→   - Windhoek urban details;
   468→   468→   - northern towns and roads;
   469→   469→   - acacia, stone, sand and open sky.
   470→   470→
   471→   471→2. **Institutional still life**
   472→   472→   - books;
   473→   473→   - notebooks;
   474→   474→   - pens;
   475→   475→   - folders and controlled records;
   476→   476→   - certificates and embossed stationery;
   477→   477→   - laptops and simple training tools;
   478→   478→   - safety equipment;
   479→   479→   - reception objects;
   480→   480→   - hospitality objects.
   481→   481→
   482→   482→3. **Learning environments**

   486→   486→   - library or reading spaces;

   488→   488→
   489→   489→4. **Architecture and material**
   490→   490→   - thresholds;
   491→   491→   - corridors;
   492→   492→   - institutional facades;
   493→   493→   - brick, plaster, metal, paper and textile;
   494→   494→   - light falling across plain surfaces.
   495→   495→
   496→   496→5. **Authentic people**
   497→   497→   - the approved founder photograph;
   498→   498→   - commissioned or properly licensed documentary photography;
   499→   499→   - real Oryx learners and staff with consent.
   500→   500→
   501→   501→### 10.2 Human imagery rules
   502→   502→
   503→   503→People are not the default visual solution.
   504→   504→
   505→   505→- Use the supplied founder photograph in the founder section.
   506→   506→- Do not generate a replacement founder or alter his age, face or identity.
   507→   507→- If the original background is distracting, make a careful cut-out and apply a restrained grade. Preserve the face, body proportions and clothing.
   508→   508→- Avoid generated close-ups of hands, crowds, smiling teams, graduation groups and posed classrooms.
   509→   509→- When generated people are unavoidable, show a distant, rear or side view with simple posture and limited visual detail.
   510→   510→- Never imply that generated people are actual learners, staff, partners or graduates.
   511→   511→
   512→   512→### 10.3 Generation prompt rules
   513→   513→
   514→   514→Generated supporting imagery must be:
   515→   515→
   516→   516→- visually simple;
   517→   517→- one subject per frame;
   518→   518→- editorial and believable;
   519→   519→- naturally lit;
   520→   520→- warm-neutral with optional maroon grading;
   521→   521→- free of visible text, logos, watermarks and signage;
   522→   522→- free of complex fingers, hand interactions or crowded faces;
   523→   523→- high resolution and suitable for the intended crop.
   524→   524→
   525→   525→Every generated asset must be visually inspected before use.
   526→   526→
   527→   527→### 10.4 Image treatment
   528→   528→
   529→   529→- Preserve natural skin tones in authentic photography.
   530→   530→- Use cream, warm grey, muted earth and maroon colour grading.
   531→   531→- Use dark maroon overlays at 20% to 65% only where required for legible text.
   532→   532→- Do not apply the same filter strength to every image.
   533→   533→- Do not use sepia so heavily that all images become brown and indistinguishable.
   534→   534→- Avoid staged stock photography and artificial campus claims.
   535→   535→
   536→   536→### 10.5 Image reuse
   537→   537→
   538→   538→- Do not repeat a hero image elsewhere on the same route or publication.
   539→   539→- Do not use one desert image as a universal background.
   540→   540→- Maintain an image register with filename, source, licence, subject, crop and pages or routes used.
   541→   541→- A repeated image must have a narrative reason, such as an opening and closing visual callback.
   542→   542→
   543→   543→## 11. Iconography and illustration
   544→   544→
   545→   545→### 11.1 Icons
   546→   546→
   547→   547→Icons are simple, institutional line drawings.
   548→   548→
   549→   549→- Base canvas: 24 × 24.
   550→   550→- Stroke: 1.5px to 2px.
   551→   551→- Colour: current text colour or brand maroon.
   552→   552→- Style: geometric, restrained and recognisable.
   553→   553→- End caps and joins must remain consistent within one family.
   554→   554→- Use labels when meaning is not universal.
   555→   555→
   556→   556→Preferred subjects include:
   557→   557→
   558→   558→- book;
   559→   559→- column;
   560→   560→- people;
   561→   561→- globe;
   562→   562→- inquiry;
   563→   563→- leadership;
   564→   564→- integrity;
   565→   565→- shield;
   566→   566→- certificate;
   567→   567→- workplace;
   568→   568→- computer;
   569→   569→- safety.
   570→   570→
   571→   571→Do not mix filled app icons, emoji, hand-drawn doodles and classical line icons in one product.
   572→   572→
   573→   573→### 11.2 Illustration
   574→   574→
   575→   575→Use editorial line or cut-paper illustration only when photography cannot communicate the idea accurately.
   576→   576→
   577→   577→- Keep scenes elementary.
   578→   578→- Use cream, maroon, black and muted earth tones.
   579→   579→- Avoid dense character scenes.
   580→   580→- Avoid cartoon mascots.
   581→   581→- Do not place generated words inside illustrations.
   582→   582→
   583→   583→## 12. Digital components
   584→   584→
   585→   585→### 12.1 Navigation
   586→   586→
   587→   587→- Navigation may float above the hero on wide screens.
   588→   588→- Use the authentic horizontal logo on light fields and the reversed version on dark photography.
   589→   589→- Desktop navigation is concise and text-led.
   590→   590→- Mobile menu icon uses two horizontal lines, not three.
   591→   591→- The icon requires an accessible name such as `Open menu`.
   592→   592→- On open, the icon changes clearly and the focus remains managed.
   593→   593→- Avoid oversized navigation pills and translucent glass panels.
   594→   594→
   595→   595→### 12.2 Hero
   596→   596→
   597→   597→The home hero may use a controlled Namibia-inspired image sequence.
   598→   598→
   599→   599→- Use three to five unique images.
   600→   600→- One visual subject per slide.
   601→   601→- Use short copy and one primary action.
   602→   602→- Do not auto-advance faster than seven seconds.
   603→   603→- Provide pause controls if motion starts automatically.
   604→   604→- Disable automatic transitions when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active.
   605→   605→- Do not place text over an uncontrolled bright or detailed area.
   606→   606→
   607→   607→### 12.3 Buttons
   608→   608→
   609→   609→#### Primary
   610→   610→
   611→   611→- Maroon background.
   612→   612→- Cream or white text.
   613→   613→- Square or 2px corners.
   614→   614→- Minimum height: 48px.
   615→   615→- Clear hover, active, focus and disabled states.
   616→   616→
   617→   617→#### Secondary
   618→   618→
   619→   619→- Transparent or cream background.
   620→   620→- Maroon or ink text.
   621→   621→- 1px to 2px border.
   622→   622→
   623→   623→#### Text action
   624→   624→
   625→   625→- Text with a restrained directional arrow.
   626→   626→- Underline on hover and keyboard focus.
   627→   627→
   628→   628→Button copy begins with a clear verb: `Explore programmes`, `Request information`, `Download profile`.
   629→   629→
   630→   630→### 12.4 Links
   631→   631→
   632→   632→- Links in running text are underlined.
   633→   633→- Colour alone must not identify a link.
   634→   634→- External links may receive a small icon and accessible description.
   635→   635→- Do not remove focus outlines.
   636→   636→
   637→   637→### 12.5 Cards
   638→   638→
   639→   639→Cards are optional, not the default page structure.
   640→   640→
   641→   641→- Use a card only when content represents a discrete object or action.
   642→   642→- Prefer image, title, short descriptor and one action.
   643→   643→- Avoid identical card grids repeated in every section.
   644→   644→- Do not use nested cards.
   645→   645→- Do not use floating glass cards over photographs.
   646→   646→
   647→   647→### 12.6 Forms
   648→   648→
   649→   649→- Labels appear above fields.
   650→   650→- Required fields are explained in text.
   651→   651→- Inputs have a minimum height of 48px.
   652→   652→- Borders remain visible in all states.
   653→   653→- Errors are shown beside the relevant field and summarised at the top.
   654→   654→- Preserve user input after validation failure.
   655→   655→- Use Source Sans 3 at 16px minimum for input text.
   656→   656→- Do not use placeholders as labels.
   657→   657→
   658→   658→### 12.7 Programme and qualification listings
   659→   659→
   660→   660→Each listing should distinguish:
   661→   661→
   662→   662→- programme or qualification title;
   663→   663→- school or division;
   664→   664→- NQF level where verified;
   665→   665→- delivery status;
   666→   666→- accreditation status;
   667→   667→- duration where approved;
   668→   668→- mode of study;
   669→   669→- entry requirements;
   670→   670→- application state.
   671→   671→
   672→   672→Never present an unapproved programme as accredited or open for enrolment. Use precise status language such as `Planned`, `Subject to accreditation`, `Applications not yet open` or `Approved`.
   673→   673→
   674→   674→### 12.8 Tables and data
   675→   675→
   676→   676→- Use real HTML tables for tabular data.
   677→   677→- Left-align text and right-align comparable numbers.
   678→   678→- Repeat table headers where relevant.
   679→   679→- Use subtle horizontal rules.
   680→   680→- Avoid heavy full-cell maroon fills in print.
   681→   681→- Provide a responsive alternative for narrow screens without changing meaning.
   682→   682→
   683→   683→### 12.9 Notices and status
   684→   684→
   685→   685→Notices use a left rule, clear heading and plain explanation.
   686→   686→
   687→   687→- Information: blue-grey.
   688→   688→- Success: green.
   689→   689→- Warning: amber-brown.
   690→   690→- Error: red.
   691→   691→
   692→   692→Do not use maroon for every status because it will lose its institutional meaning.
   693→   693→
   694→   694→## 13. Interaction states
   695→   695→
   696→   696→Every interactive component must define:
   697→   697→
   698→   698→- default;
   699→   699→- hover;
   700→   700→- active or pressed;
   701→   701→- keyboard focus;
   702→   702→- disabled;
   703→   703→- loading;
   704→   704→- success;
   705→   705→- validation failure;
   706→   706→- server failure;
   707→   707→- timeout.
   708→   708→
   709→   709→Every route or system should account for:
   710→   710→
   711→   711→- empty state;
   712→   712→- no results;
   713→   713→- offline;
   714→   714→- maintenance;
   715→   715→- access denied;
   716→   716→- session expired;
   717→   717→- locked preview;
   718→   718→- 404;
   719→   719→- 500.
   720→   720→
   721→   721→### 13.1 Focus
   722→   722→
   723→   723→Use a clearly visible two-layer focus treatment:
   724→   724→
   725→   725→```css
   726→   726→:focus-visible {
   727→   727→  outline: 3px solid #FFF8EF;
   728→   728→  box-shadow: 0 0 0 5px #171717;
   729→   729→}
   730→   730→```
   731→   731→
   732→   732→Reverse the colours where needed so the indicator remains visible. Focus must not be hidden behind sticky headers or overlays.
   733→   733→
   734→   734→### 13.2 Loading
   735→   735→
   736→   736→- Prefer content-shaped skeletons only when the wait is short and structure is known.
   737→   737→- Use text such as `Loading programmes` for important processes.
   738→   738→- Never use an endless spinner without an explanation or timeout path.
   739→   739→
   740→   740→## 14. Motion
   741→   741→
   742→   742→Motion is restrained and functional.
   743→   743→
   744→   744→```css
   745→   745→--duration-fast: 120ms;
   746→   746→--duration-standard: 200ms;
   747→   747→--duration-slow: 360ms;
   748→   748→--ease-standard: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1);
   749→   749→```
   750→   750→
   751→   751→Approved motion:
   752→   752→
   753→   753→- opacity change;
   754→   754→- short vertical reveal;
   755→   755→- subtle image crossfade;
   756→   756→- menu opening;
   757→   757→- focus and state transitions.
   758→   758→
   759→   759→Avoid:
   760→   760→
   761→   761→- continuous floating;
   762→   762→- parallax;
   763→   763→- scroll hijacking;
   764→   764→- bouncing icons;
   765→   765→- decorative counters;
   766→   766→- rotating words;
   767→   767→- large text that follows the pointer.
   768→   768→
   769→   769→Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Essential information must not depend on animation.
   770→   770→
   771→   771→## 15. Responsive behaviour
   772→   772→
   773→   773→- Design mobile first.
   774→   774→- Preserve reading order when layouts collapse.
   775→   775→- Do not hide essential programme, fee, status or contact information on mobile.
   776→   776→- Convert multi-column editorial sections to one coherent column.
   777→   777→- Crop images around the same primary subject at every breakpoint.
   778→   778→- Keep touch targets at least 44 × 44 CSS px as an Oryx internal standard.
   779→   779→- Avoid horizontal scrolling except for clearly labelled data regions.
   780→   780→- Do not reduce body type merely to preserve a desktop composition.
   781→   781→
   782→   782→## 16. Accessibility
   783→   783→
   784→   784→The digital target is WCAG 2.2 AA.
   785→   785→
   786→   786→Minimum requirements:
   787→   787→
   788→   788→- Normal text contrast: 4.5:1.
   789→   789→- Large text contrast: 3:1.
   790→   790→- Interface and meaningful graphic contrast: 3:1.
   791→   791→- Keyboard access for every interactive function.
   792→   792→- Visible and unobscured focus.
   793→   793→- Logical heading order.
   794→   794→- Semantic landmarks.
   795→   795→- Descriptive link text.
   796→   796→- Useful alt text for informative images.
   797→   797→- Empty alt text for decorative images.
   798→   798→- Captions or transcripts for meaningful audio and video.
   799→   799→- Labels, instructions and errors that do not rely on colour alone.
   800→   800→- Zoom and reflow without lost content.
   801→   801→- Reduced-motion support.
   802→   802→
   803→   803→Oryx should exceed the minimum where practical. The approved core colour pairings in section 6.3 already provide strong text contrast.
   804→   804→
   805→   805→## 17. Editorial and print system
   806→   806→
   807→   807→### 17.1 Formats
   808→   808→
   809→   809→- Primary institutional documents: A4 portrait.
   810→   810→- Editorial spreads and print masters: A3 landscape folded to A4 where planned.
   811→   811→- Posters: A3, A2 or larger.
   812→   812→- Social crops are derived from a master composition, not stretched from print pages.
   813→   813→
   814→   814→### 17.2 Production
   815→   815→
   816→   816→- Raster images: 300 ppi at final print size.
   817→   817→- Preferred logo format: vector PDF, SVG or EPS.
   818→   818→- Bleed: 3 mm unless the printer specifies otherwise.
   819→   819→- Safe area: at least 12 mm from trim; 15 mm preferred for binding.
   820→   820→- Keep essential content away from folds and gutters.
   821→   821→- Embed fonts only where licences permit.
   822→   822→- Export a press PDF using the printer's requested PDF/X profile.
   823→   823→- Obtain the printer's CMYK profile before final colour conversion.
   824→   824→- Review a physical proof before a high-volume run.
   825→   825→
   826→   826→### 17.3 Print-friendly design
   827→   827→
   828→   828→- Cream and white are the default page fields.
   829→   829→- Reserve full maroon pages for covers, section openers and deliberate moments.
   830→   830→- Use maroon rules, headings and small fields instead of ink-heavy boxes.
   831→   831→- Do not place the maroon logo on maroon.
   832→   832→- Maintain readable tonal contrast when printed in greyscale.
   833→   833→- Avoid very light body text.
   834→   834→
   835→   835→### 17.4 Editorial rhythm
   836→   836→
   837→   837→A profile or prospectus should vary its page architecture:
   838→   838→
   839→   839→- image-led opener;
   840→   840→- quiet mission page;
   841→   841→- structured programme overview;
   842→   842→- full-width object image;
   843→   843→- data or process spread;
   844→   844→- founder page using the authentic supplied photograph;
   845→   845→- closing contact page.
   846→   846→
   847→   847→Do not repeat one template across every page. Do not repeat images unless the repetition is a deliberate narrative callback.
   848→   848→
   849→   849→## 18. Physical environment
   850→   850→
   851→   851→The spatial design follows the same principles.
   852→   852→
   853→   853→- Cream or warm off-white walls.
   854→   854→- Maroon used at entrances, wayfinding, key doors or controlled feature areas.
   855→   855→- Ink-black text and signage.
   856→   856→- Honest materials such as sealed brick, plaster, timber, powder-coated steel and corrugated metal used carefully.
   857→   857→- Clear sightlines and uncluttered reception.
   858→   858→- Real learner notices separated from permanent brand signage.
   859→   859→- Accessible routes and legible directional signs.
   860→   860→
   861→   861→Avoid covering walls with oversized motivational quotes, repeated logos or decorative gradients.
   862→   862→
   863→   863→## 19. Content and voice
   864→   864→
   865→   865→### 19.1 Voice
   866→   866→
   867→   867→Oryx sounds:
   868→   868→
   869→   869→- clear;
   870→   870→- calm;
   871→   871→- capable;
   872→   872→- evidence-led;
   873→   873→- respectful;
   874→   874→- ambitious without exaggeration.
   875→   875→
   876→   876→### 19.2 Writing rules
   877→   877→
   878→   878→- Lead with the useful fact.
   879→   879→- Use short sentences and concrete verbs.
   880→   880→- Explain acronyms on first use.
   881→   881→- Distinguish current facts, approved plans and assumptions.
   882→   882→- Use Namibian English consistently.
   883→   883→- Use `recognised`, `programme`, `organisation` and `labour`.
   884→   884→- Use `N$` for Namibia dollars.
   885→   885→- Use an en dash for numeric ranges where supported.
   886→   886→- Avoid em dashes in public copy.
   887→   887→
   888→   888→### 19.3 Prohibited language
   889→   889→
   890→   890→Do not use unsupported superlatives or generic AI copy such as:
   891→   891→
   892→   892→- revolutionise;
   893→   893→- unlock;
   894→   894→- next generation;
   895→   895→- cutting edge;
   896→   896→- seamless;
   897→   897→- game changing;
   898→   898→- world class;
   899→   899→- unwavering commitment.
   900→   900→
   901→   901→Do not invent:
   902→   902→
   903→   903→- accreditations;
   904→   904→- learner numbers;
   905→   905→- placement rates;
   906→   906→- partners;
   907→   907→- testimonials;
   908→   908→- prices;
   909→   909→- dates;
   910→   910→- addresses;
   911→   911→- registration details;
   912→   912→- funding approvals;
   913→   913→- business results.
   914→   914→
   915→   915→### 19.4 AI language
   916→   916→
   917→   917→AI is an embedded productivity and responsibility layer, not the identity of every qualification. Explain what a learner will do with a tool and how work will be verified. Do not market familiar careers as experimental AI courses.
   918→   918→
   919→   919→## 20. Data visualisation
   920→   920→
   921→   921→- Use charts only when they communicate a relationship more clearly than prose or a table.
   922→   922→- Start with maroon, dark maroon, ink and warm neutrals.
   923→   923→- Add status colours only when needed.
   924→   924→- Label values directly where space permits.
   925→   925→- Do not rely on colour alone.
   926→   926→- Avoid 3D charts, decorative gauges and fake dashboards.
   927→   927→- Cite the source, period and methodology.
   928→   928→- Mark projections and assumptions visibly.

  1007→  1007→
  1008→  1008→| Correct | Incorrect |
  1009→  1009→|---|---|
  1010→  1010→| Authentic logo overlaid after image production | AI-generated or redrawn logo |
  1011→  1011→| Reversed cream logo on maroon | Maroon logo on maroon |
  1012→  1012→| One distinct image per major section | Same image repeated as filler |
  1013→  1013→| Books, pens, tools, architecture and landscape | Generic smiling team stock |
  1014→  1014→| Authentic founder photograph | Generated or age-altered founder |
  1015→  1015→| Trajan for short display titles | Trajan for long paragraphs |
  1016→  1016→| Source Sans 3 for interfaces | Multiple unrelated body fonts |
  1017→  1017→| Restrained gradient for depth or contrast | Purple, neon or glossy gradient |
  1018→  1018→| Rectilinear composition and sampled diagonal motif | Random blobs and decorative slashes |
  1019→  1019→| Clear status language | Unverified accreditation or enrolment claim |
  1020→  1020→| Varied editorial page architecture | One repeated card template |
  1021→  1021→| Visible keyboard focus | Removed focus outline |
  1022→  1022→| Cream-led print pages | Ink-heavy maroon boxes on every page |

  1025→  1025→
  1026→  1026→Before approval, confirm:
  1027→  1027→
  1028→  1028→### Brand
  1029→  1029→
  1030→  1030→- [ ] The logo is an authentic supplied asset.
  1031→  1031→- [ ] The correct logo variation is used for the background.
  1032→  1032→- [ ] The mark is not regenerated, stretched or recoloured.
  1033→  1033→- [ ] Institution and division names follow the approved hierarchy.
  1034→  1034→
  1035→  1035→### Typography
  1036→  1036→
  1037→  1037→- [ ] Trajan is licensed or replaced consistently by Cinzel.
  1038→  1038→- [ ] Source Sans 3 is used for body and interface text.
  1039→  1039→- [ ] Body text remains readable at the intended size.
  1040→  1040→- [ ] Line length and hierarchy are controlled.
  1041→  1041→
  1042→  1042→### Colour
  1043→  1043→
  1044→  1044→- [ ] Core brand colours match the exact hex values.
  1045→  1045→- [ ] Text and controls pass WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
  1046→  1046→- [ ] Status is not communicated by colour alone.
  1047→  1047→- [ ] Gradients are restrained and purposeful.
  1048→  1048→
  1049→  1049→### Imagery
  1050→  1050→
  1051→  1051→- [ ] No image is repeated without a narrative reason.
  1052→  1052→- [ ] Generated images contain no logo or important text.
  1053→  1053→- [ ] Human imagery is authentic, distant or visually simple.
  1054→  1054→- [ ] The founder is represented only by the supplied approved photograph.
  1055→  1055→- [ ] Hands, crowds and AI-looking details have been inspected.
  1056→  1056→
  1057→  1057→### Layout
  1058→  1058→
  1059→  1059→- [ ] There is one dominant message per composition.
  1060→  1060→- [ ] The design uses a coherent grid and spacing scale.
  1061→  1061→- [ ] Mobile reading order is logical.
  1062→  1062→- [ ] Cards, pills, gradients and shadows are not overused.
  1063→  1063→
  1064→  1064→### Content
  1065→  1065→
  1066→  1066→- [ ] All facts and statuses are verified.
  1067→  1067→- [ ] Planned items are labelled as planned.
  1068→  1068→- [ ] The current email is `contact@oryxinstitute.org`.
  1069→  1069→- [ ] No fake metrics, testimonials, partners or accreditations appear.
  1070→  1070→- [ ] Public copy avoids prohibited generic language.
  1071→  1071→
  1072→  1072→### Accessibility and function
  1073→  1073→
  1074→  1074→- [ ] Keyboard navigation works.
  1075→  1075→- [ ] Focus is visible and unobscured.
  1076→  1076→- [ ] Images have correct alt treatment.
  1077→  1077→- [ ] Forms expose labels and errors.
  1078→  1078→- [ ] Reduced-motion behaviour works.
