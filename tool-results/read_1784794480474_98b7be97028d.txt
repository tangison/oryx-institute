     1→# BRAND.md - Oryx Institute
     2→
     3→> The complete brand identity for Oryx Institute. This document is the source of truth for every brand decision on the website and on every other Oryx Institute touchpoint. The public expression of this identity lives at `/brand`. Confidential internal rules are excluded from the public page.

     7→## 1. Brand purpose
     8→
     9→Oryx Institute exists to give Namibians practical, recognised skills through vocational education rooted in the Namibian landscape, work, and dignity. The institution trains, assesses, researches, and advises across multiple disciplines, serving learners, employers, and the wider Namibian economy.
    10→
    11→The institution is being established. Its purpose is durable: it will outlast the pre-launch phase and continue to guide the institution's public website without redesign.
    12→
    13→## 2. Brand audience
    14→
    15→(See PRODUCT.md section 2 for the full audience list.) Primary: prospective learners, employers, WIL partners, corporate training clients, research and advisory clients, funding and institutional partners, investors, the Namibian public. The brand voice addresses all of these audiences with the same restrained, credible, Namibian register. It does not adjust tone per audience segment.
    16→
    17→## 3. Brand position
    18→
    19→Oryx Institute is positioned as a quietly prestigious Namibian institution. It is academic in character and vocational in practice. It is rooted in Windhoek and the wider Namibian landscape. It is not a technology startup, not a school template, not a corporate training mill, not an international franchise.
    20→
    21→The institution differentiates on place, discipline, restraint, honesty, multiple learning pathways, and original commissioned photography of Namibia.
    22→
    23→## 4. Brand promise
    24→
    25→**Knowledge shaped by the Namibian landscape.**
    26→
    27→This is the single brand promise. It is not a tagline to be repeated on every page. It appears in the brand book, on the `/brand` page, and as the conceptual anchor for photography, copy, and layout decisions. It does not appear in body copy, navigation, or calls to action.
    28→
    29→## 5. Brand personality
    30→
    31→Five traits, in order of weight:
    32→
    33→1. **Quietly prestigious.** Confident without volume. Serious without severity. Earns attention rather than demanding it.
    34→2. **Academic but contemporary.** Disciplined like a research institution, current like a magazine, not nostalgic, not retro.
    35→3. **Minimal.** Negative space is content. Restraint is a feature, not a limitation.
    36→4. **Warm.** Cream, sand, soft light, human hands, ordinary objects. Not cold, not corporate, not clinical.
    37→5. **Architectural.** Grids, rules, proportion, asymmetry. Composed, not decorated.
    38→
    39→## 6. Brand voice
    40→
    41→- Sparse. Every word earns its place.
    42→- Accurate. Claims match verified facts. Unknown information is labelled "To be confirmed".
    43→- Credible. No puffery, no superlatives, no inflated language.
    44→- Namibian. Direct, practical, dignified. Not American corporate, not British academic, not African stereotyped.
    45→- Active voice preferred. Passive voice used only when the actor is genuinely irrelevant.
    46→- Short sentences for emphasis. Longer sentences for explanation. Variation is intentional.
    47→- Numbers and names spelled out when first mentioned. Abbreviations only when established.
    48→- Headlines are noun phrases or short statements, not commands or questions.
    49→- Calls to action are verbs: Register Interest, Explore the Institute, View Programmes, Submit Enquiry.
    50→- Captions explain what an image shows and why it matters. They do not decorate.

   145→## 9. Logo rules
   146→
   147→### 9.1 Clear space
   148→
   149→The logo requires clear space on all sides equal to the height of the oryx symbol within the logo (the oryx figure, not the full logo height). No other graphic element, text, image edge, or colour boundary may enter this clear space.
   150→
   151→For the 612x408 px supplied logo, the oryx symbol occupies approximately the left 40% of the logo. Its height within the logo is approximately 280 pixels. Clear space minimum: 280 pixels on all sides when used at full supplied size. Scale proportionally for smaller uses.
   152→
   153→### 9.2 Minimum size
   154→
   155→- **Digital, primary logo:** 160 pixels wide. Below this size the wordmark becomes illegible.
   156→- **Digital, shield icon:** 32 x 42 pixels (favicon size). The shield remains legible at small sizes due to its bold silhouette.
   157→- **Print, primary logo:** 30 mm wide. Below this size the wordmark becomes illegible in print.
   158→- **Print, shield icon:** 8 mm wide minimum.
   159→
   160→### 9.3 Background rules
   161→
   162→**Approved backgrounds:**
   163→
   164→- Cream `#FCFBF9` (primary background).
   165→- Warm White `#EADCCD` (secondary background).
   166→- Pure white `#FFFFFF` (when cream is not available, e.g. some email clients).
   167→- Sand `#D3C2B1` (when contrast is verified, see section 10).
   168→- Light marble or stone surfaces in supplied photography (when the logo is placed in a clear-space region with sufficient contrast).
   169→
   170→**Prohibited backgrounds:**
   171→
   172→- Maroon `#721220` (the logo's maroon illustration would be invisible; do not recolour the logo).
   173→- Deep Maroon `#4A231B` (same issue).
   174→- Black `#0F0E0D`.
   175→- Any background where the contrast between the logo ink and the background falls below WCAG 2.2 AA for non-text graphical objects (3:1).
   176→- Busy photographic backgrounds without a clear-space cream or white panel behind the logo.
   177→- Gradient backgrounds.
   178→- Patterned backgrounds.
   179→- Other brand colours as backgrounds.
   180→
   181→### 9.4 Misuse rules
   182→
   183→The following are prohibited. Each is a brand violation:
   184→
   185→1. Stretching or compressing the logo vertically or horizontally. Always preserve the 3:2 aspect ratio.
   186→2. Rotating the logo.
   187→3. Recolouring any part of the logo. The maroon illustration and black wordmark are fixed.
   188→4. Adding drop shadows, glows, outlines, or any effect to the logo.
   189→5. Placing the logo over a busy image without a clear-space panel.
   190→6. Placing the logo on a background where it lacks contrast.
   191→7. Cropping the logo so the oryx symbol or the wordmark is cut.
   192→8. Rearranging the logo elements.
   193→9. Replacing the logo typeface.
   194→10. Animating the logo (no pulsing, fading, rotating, or scaling animations).
   195→11. Inserting the logo into a generated photograph or illustration.
   196→12. Using the logo as a watermark or background pattern.
   197→13. Using the shield icon where the primary logo is required (e.g. header).
   198→14. Using the primary logo where the shield icon is required (e.g. favicon).
   199→15. Redrawing, retracing, or recreating the logo in vector or any other format.
   200→16. Adding a tagline or strapline to the logo.
   201→17. Adding a stroke or fill around the logo.
   202→18. Placing the logo inside a shape (circle, square, badge).
   203→19. Distorting the logo with perspective, skew, or 3D effects.
   204→20. Using a low-resolution version of the logo below 2x the display size.

   278→## 11. Typography
   279→
   280→### 11.1 Type system
   281→
   282→The brief requires "editorial serif display typography paired with a restrained sans-serif". The brief also rejects defaulting to Inter as the primary typeface.
   283→
   284→The system uses two type families:
   285→
   286→- **Display serif:** for headlines, section titles, hero copy, and editorial emphasis. A high-contrast contemporary serif with editorial character.
   287→- **Body sans-serif:** for body copy, navigation, buttons, captions, form labels, and UI text. A restrained, neutral sans-serif with strong legibility at small sizes.
   288→
   289→### 11.2 Candidate fonts (need user confirmation)
   290→
   291→Three display serif candidates and three body sans-serif candidates are presented for user selection. Each pair has been evaluated for editorial character, Namibian cultural appropriateness (i.e. does not feel American, British, or European-specific), accessibility, performance, and licensing.
   292→
   293→**Display serif candidates:**
   294→
   295→1. **Fraunces** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). High-contrast contemporary serif with variable optical sizes. Strong editorial character. Performs well at display sizes. Pairs naturally with a neutral sans-serif. Recommended default.
   296→2. **Newsreader** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). Editorial serif designed for reading news. Softer than Fraunces, more legible at small sizes. Less distinctive at display sizes.
   297→3. **Source Serif 4** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). Adobe's editorial serif. Neutral and well-engineered. Less characterful than Fraunces but more conservative.
   298→
   299→**Body sans-serif candidates:**
   300→
   301→1. **Inter** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). The brief rejects Inter as the primary typeface but allows it as the body sans-serif when paired with a strong serif display. Excellent legibility at small sizes. Variable font for performance. Recommended default.
   302→2. **IBM Plex Sans** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). More characterful than Inter. Pairs well with editorial serifs. Slightly heavier file size.
   303→3. **Source Sans 3** (Open Font License, Google Fonts). Adobe's neutral sans-serif. Conservative and well-engineered.
   304→
   305→**Recommended pairing:** Fraunces (display) + Inter (body). This pairing delivers editorial character at display sizes, neutral legibility at body sizes, both as variable fonts for performance, both under permissive licenses, both available on Google Fonts with no licensing cost.
   306→
   307→**Fallback stack:** For each selected font, the fallback stack is:
   308→
   309→- Display serif: `'Fraunces', 'Newsreader', 'Source Serif 4', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`
   310→- Body sans-serif: `'Inter', 'IBM Plex Sans', 'Source Sans 3', system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif`
   311→
   312→The fallback stack ensures the website renders correctly even when web fonts fail to load.
   313→

   330→### 11.4 Typography rules
   331→
   332→- Headlines use the display serif at regular weight (400). Bold weight (700) is reserved for emphasis within headlines and for card titles.
   333→- Body copy uses the body sans-serif at regular weight (400). Bold weight (700) is used for strong emphasis, never for entire paragraphs.
   334→- Italic is used for editorial emphasis in body copy and for photo captions. It is not used in headlines.
   335→- Underline is reserved for links. Underline is removed on hover for primary navigation and buttons.
   336→- Uppercase is reserved for eyebrow labels and form labels. Never use uppercase for headlines or body copy.
   337→- Letter spacing is tightened on large display sizes and loosened on small uppercase labels.
   338→- Line height is generous (1.6-1.65) for body copy to support reading.
   339→- Line height is tight (1.05-1.15) for display headlines to preserve editorial composition.
   340→- Maximum line length for body copy: 65-75 characters. Longer lines reduce readability.
   341→- Numerals: use tabular numerals (tnum) in tables and data displays. Use proportional numerals (pnum) in body copy and headlines.
   342→- Smart quotes and apostrophes are used in all rendered text. Straight quotes are used only in code.

   350→## 12. Grid, spacing, and layout
   351→
   352→### 12.1 Grid
   353→
   354→- **Desktop:** 12-column grid, 80 px maximum column width, 24 px gutter, 96 px outer margin on a 1440 px viewport. On wider viewports, the grid is centred with a maximum content width of 1440 px.
   355→- **Tablet:** 8-column grid, 32 px gutter, 48 px outer margin on a 768 px viewport.
   356→- **Mobile:** 4-column grid, 16 px gutter, 24 px outer margin on a 375 px viewport.
   357→
   358→The grid is used as a structural guide, not as a rigid container. Deliberate asymmetry is encouraged: images and text blocks span different column counts to create editorial rhythm.
   359→
   360→### 12.2 Spacing scale
   361→
   362→A modular scale based on 4 px:
   363→
   364→| Token | Value | Use |
   365→|---|---|---|
   366→| `space-0` | 0 | None |
   367→| `space-1` | 4 px | Tight inline spacing |
   368→| `space-2` | 8 px | Inline element spacing |
   369→| `space-3` | 12 px | Tight vertical spacing |
   370→| `space-4` | 16 px | Default vertical spacing |
   371→| `space-5` | 24 px | Component internal padding |
   372→| `space-6` | 32 px | Component vertical spacing |
   373→| `space-7` | 48 px | Section internal vertical spacing |
   374→| `space-8` | 64 px | Section vertical spacing |
   375→| `space-9` | 96 px | Section vertical spacing, desktop |
   376→| `space-10` | 128 px | Major section transition, desktop |
   377→| `space-11` | 160 px | Hero spacing, desktop |
   378→
   379→### 12.3 Layout rules
   380→
   381→- Large areas of negative space are content. Do not fill them.
   382→- Fine rules (1 px) in Stone `#BFA68F` are used to divide sections, not heavy lines or boxes.
   383→- Deliberate asymmetry: a 7/5 or 5/7 column split is preferred over a 6/6 split for image-and-text sections.
   384→- No card grids of identical cards. Use editorial image panels of varying sizes.
   385→- No glassmorphism, no frosted panels, no blur effects.
   386→- No blob shapes, no decorative SVG ornaments, no random gradient overlays.
   387→- Sections are separated by vertical spacing, not by borders or backgrounds. Background colour changes (e.g. Cream to Warm White) are used sparingly to create surface rhythm.

   389→## 13. Shapes, radius, borders, shadows
   390→
   391→- **Corner radius:** 0 px default. The brand is architectural and sharp. Rounded corners are prohibited except for form inputs (4 px) and buttons (0 px, sharp). Images are never rounded. Cards are never rounded.
   392→- **Borders:** 1 px solid in Stone `#BFA68F` for dividers. 1 px solid in Oryx Maroon `#721220` for active form fields and focus states. No thick borders.
   393→- **Shadows:** Prohibited. The brand is flat and architectural. Depth is created through colour, spacing, and surface rhythm, not through shadows. The only exception is the floating navigation bar, which uses a single subtle shadow `0 1px 0 rgba(15, 14, 13, 0.04), 0 8px 24px rgba(15, 14, 13, 0.06)` to lift it above hero images. No other shadows anywhere.
   394→- **Texture:** Prohibited. No grain, no noise, no paper textures, no fabric textures. Surfaces are flat colour.

   396→## 14. Imagery and photography
   397→
   398→### 14.1 Photographic treatment
   399→
   400→All original photography on the website follows a single, consistent treatment:
   401→
   402→- **Warm editorial realism.** Naturalistic, not stylised.
   403→- **Cream and sandstone colour cast.** White balance shifted slightly warm.
   404→- **Muted maroon accents.** Maroon appears as a detail colour in objects, fabric, and surfaces, never as a dominant colour.
   405→- **Soft sepia undertone.** Subtle warm desaturation in shadows.
   406→- **Strong but natural directional sunlight.** Long shadows, soft highlights, no harsh contrast.
   407→- **Minimal compositions.** One main subject per image. Generous negative space.
   408→- **High-detail surfaces.** Stone, paper, fabric, wood, sand, plaster.
   409→- **No text in images.** No signs, no documents with readable text, no notebooks with readable handwriting.
   410→- **No logos in images.** No Oryx Institute logo placed in scenes.
   411→- **No fantasy architecture.** Real Namibian buildings and plausible Windhoek architectural concepts only.
   412→- **No visibly artificial faces or hands.** If a person is shown, hands and faces are natural and dignified. If AI generation cannot achieve this, the image is recomposed to show hands only or no person.
   413→- **Namibian people look natural, dignified, and contemporary.** Not staged, not costumed, not exoticised.
   414→
   415→### 14.2 Image categories
   416→
   417→The website uses five categories of original imagery:
   418→
   419→1. **Landscape heroes.** Oryx, dunes, camel-thorn trees, mountain landscapes. Wide aspect ratio (3:2 or 16:9). Used for full-viewport hero slider and section backgrounds.
   420→2. **Learning objects.** Books, notebooks, laptops, desks, pencils, calculators, files. Still life compositions. Used for programme and pathway sections.
   421→3. **Architectural concepts.** Minimal Windhoek training-centre interiors and exteriors. Used for campus section. Always captioned as architectural concept.
   422→4. **Learning moments.** Learners, trainers, assessors, mentors shown in natural learning contexts. Hands preferred over faces. Side and back views preferred over direct eye contact.
   423→5. **Abstract details.** Close-ups of Namibian stone, maroon fabric, paper textures, sand surfaces. Used as section accents and editorial transitions.
   424→
   425→### 14.3 Image generation rules
   426→
   427→Each image is generated separately. Each generation prompt specifies:
   428→
   429→- One main subject.
   430→- One controlled environment.
   431→- A generous amount of negative space.
   432→- The consistent photographic treatment defined above.
   433→- A specific aspect ratio.
   434→- A specific filename and alt text.
   435→
   436→The image manifest in `BUILD_PLAN.md` records every image with prompt, route, section, aspect ratio, filename, alt text, and generation status.
   437→
   438→### 14.4 Image technical rules
   439→
   440→- **Format:** AVIF primary, WebP fallback. JPEG fallback only when AVIF and WebP are not supported.
   441→- **Responsive srcsets:** Generated automatically by Next.js Image component.
   442→- **Lazy loading:** Below-the-fold images use `loading="lazy"`. Above-the-fold images use `priority`.
   443→- **First hero image:** Receives `priority` loading to ensure fast LCP.
   444→- **Aspect ratios:** Preserved exactly. Never stretched.
   445→- **Crops:** Generated for responsive breakpoints (e.g. a 3:2 landscape image may have a 4:5 portrait crop for mobile). Original aspect ratio is never distorted.
   446→- **Alt text:** Every image has descriptive alt text. Decorative images use empty alt `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"`.
   447→
   448→### 14.5 Prohibited imagery
   449→
   450→- Stock photography from any stock service.
   451→- Staged corporate photographs (people shaking hands, people pointing at laptops, people smiling at cameras).
   452→- Mortarboard cliches (graduation caps repeated in every section). One graduation cap image is allowed in the hero slider.
   453→- Pyramids or unrelated classical symbols.
   454→- Generic AI startup illustrations (geometric shapes, abstract gradients, character illustrations).
   455→- Fantasy architecture (impossible buildings, futuristic structures).
   456→- Random blob shapes.
   457→- Generated text or logos inside images.
   458→- Oversized maroon blocks across every section.
   459→- Decorative dashboards (fake analytics, fake charts, fake metrics).
   460→- Crowded compositions.
   461→- Visibly artificial faces or hands.

   463→## 15. Icon system
   464→
   465→- **Style:** Single-weight (1.5 px stroke), single-colour (Ink Black or Oryx Maroon), no fills, no rounded corners on stroke ends.
   466→- **Source:** Custom SVG icons designed for Oryx Institute. No icon library (Lucide, Heroicons, Feather) is used because their visual character conflicts with the editorial brand.
   467→- **Size:** 16 px, 20 px, 24 px, 32 px. Larger sizes for feature icons.
   468→- **Use:** Functional icons only (e.g. arrow, check, close, menu, search, external link). Decorative icons are prohibited.
   469→- **Logo as icon:** The shield icon is the only brand mark used as an icon (favicon, app icon, social avatar). It is not used as a decorative element in body content.

   471→## 16. Motion principles
   472→
   473→### 16.1 Motion character
   474→
   475→- **Slow, cinematic, restrained.** Transitions take time. They do not rush.
   476→- **Naturalistic.** Easing curves mimic physical motion. No bouncy or springy easing.
   477→- **Purposeful.** Motion communicates a change in state or context. It does not decorate.
   478→- **Subtle.** Opacity, transform, and clip-path are the primary motion properties. No filter animations, no shadow animations.
   479→
   480→### 16.2 Easing and duration
   481→
   482→| Token | Duration | Easing | Use |
   483→|---|---|---|---|
   484→| `motion-instant` | 0 ms | linear | State changes that should feel immediate |
   485→| `motion-fast` | 150 ms | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | Hover states, focus states, button presses |
   486→| `motion-medium` | 300 ms | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | Tooltip, dropdown, small panel transitions |
   487→| `motion-slow` | 600 ms | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | Hero slider transitions, large panel transitions |
   488→| `motion-cinematic` | 1200 ms | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | Page entrance, hero text fade-in |
   489→
   490→### 16.3 Reduced motion
   491→
   492→All motion respects the user's `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` setting. When reduced motion is requested:
   493→
   494→- All transitions are reduced to 0 ms or replaced with instant state changes.
   495→- The hero slider does not auto-advance. Manual controls remain functional.
   496→- Parallax effects are disabled.
   497→- Fade-in animations are replaced with instant visibility.
   498→
   499→Reduced motion is not a degraded experience. It is an alternative experience with equal information density.
   500→
   501→### 16.4 Prohibited motion
   502→
   503→- Auto-playing video.
   504→- Marquee or continuously scrolling content.
   505→- Pulsing, blinking, or strobing effects.
   506→- Animations on the logo.
   507→- Animations that draw attention away from content.
   508→- Animations that interfere with reading or interaction.
   509→- Animations that consume significant CPU or battery.

   511→## 17. Component principles
   512→
   513→### 17.1 Navigation (floating bar)
   514→
   515→- **Surface:** Cream `#FCFBF9` with 90% opacity, or warm white with 90% opacity. Subtle 1 px bottom border in Stone `#BFA68F`. Subtle shadow (see section 13).
   516→- **Layout:** Original primary logo on the left. "Register Interest" action on the right (desktop). Two-line menu icon on the far right.
   517→- **Menu icon:** Exactly two horizontal lines, not three. Lines are 20 px wide, 2 px tall, 6 px apart. Active state: lines rotate to form an X.
   518→- **Compact behaviour:** On scroll, the navigation bar reduces in height and the logo scales down. The surface opacity increases to 95%.
   519→- **Keyboard accessibility:** Tab order: logo, primary nav, Register Interest, menu toggle. Focus visible (2 px outline in Oryx Maroon). Escape closes the menu. Enter and space toggle the menu.
   520→- **Mobile:** Two-line icon preserved. "Register Interest" action becomes a smaller button or icon. Menu opens as a calm full-screen composition, not a dropdown.

   522→### 17.2 Open menu (full-screen editorial composition)
   523→
   524→- **Surface:** Full viewport. Cream `#FCFBF9` background.
   525→- **Layout:** Two-column. Left column: large editorial navigation list. Right column: featured photograph and current pre-launch notice.
   526→- **Navigation list:** Institute, Schools, Programmes, RPL and WIL, Research and Advisory, Campus, Updates, Admissions, Contact. Display serif at 48-60 px. Vertical rhythm 1.4 line height. Hover: maroon colour, slight horizontal shift.

   529→- **Animation:** Slides in from the right over 600 ms with the cinematic easing. Reduces to instant on `prefers-reduced-motion`.

   577→## 18. Operational states
   578→
   579→Every interactive surface must communicate its operational state. The website implements:
   580→
   581→- **Loading:** Skeleton screens in Sand `#D3C2B1`. No spinners for full-page loads. Spinners for inline submissions only.
   582→- **Empty:** Clear empty-state copy explaining what is empty and what to do next. Example: Updates feed shows "No updates yet. Verified institutional updates will appear here as the institution is established."
   583→- **Success:** Confirmation page or inline confirmation. Clear next steps. No generic "Thank you" pages.
   584→- **Validation failure:** Inline error messages. Focus moves to the first invalid field. Aria-live announcement.
   585→- **Server failure:** 500 page with clear copy and a way back to safety (home, contact).
   586→- **Timeout:** 408 page or inline timeout message with retry option.
   587→- **No results:** Empty search results, empty filter results. Suggest alternative actions.
   588→- **Offline:** Service worker offline page. Clear copy explaining the offline state.
   589→- **Maintenance:** 503 page with clear copy and expected restoration time when known.
   590→- **Access denied:** 403 page. Clear copy explaining why access is denied and what to do.
   591→- **Session expired:** 419 page. Clear copy and re-authentication path.
   592→- **Locked demo:** Intentional preview state for locked routes in demo mode. Explains that the page is part of the planned site and will be unlocked.
   593→- **404:** Clear copy. Suggests the most likely destinations. Search when available.
   594→- **500:** Clear copy. Apologises without grovelling. Provides contact link.
