## 1. What Oryx is (the one-paragraph version)

Oryx Politechnical Institute (理工, "science and engineering") is a startup
polytechnic for Windhoek, Namibia. It teaches science, engineering, technology
and applied problem-solving, and it is AI-native from day one: the tools are
not a lab at the back of the campus, they are the campus. The institute's
model takes the Stanford recipe, a world-class teaching core, an
entrepreneurship spine in every programme, and a venture ecosystem around it,
and compresses it for a Namibian startup: small, fast, practitioner-taught,
and built so that every graduate leaves with something that works and
something that sells.

**Motto:** "A man is only as good as his tools. The brain is the biggest tool."

The motto is the strategy. It justifies the AI-native positioning (we teach
with the biggest tool there is), the entrepreneurship integration (a venture
is a tool), and the published tools list (section 5.3). It belongs on the
site, in the prospectus, and on every certificate's back face.

## 2. The market, with the real numbers

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Youth unemployment (15–24), 2025 | 38.05% | ILO/Statista, FRED series |
| Youth aged 15–34 in Namibia | 1,018,529 | NSA labour survey, Jan 2025 |
| Of those: out of the labour force | 563,499 | NSA labour survey, Jan 2025 |
| Of those: unemployed | 202,144 | NSA labour survey, Jan 2025 |
| TVET enrolment target (NTA strategic plan) | 45,000 | World Bank Skills Policy Note |
| New TVET trainees enrolled, latest reported intake | 13,500 | NTA, Mar 2026 |
| VET levy on employers, in force since | 2014 (VET Act 2008) | UNESCO-UNEVOC TVET profile |
| NUST student body (formerly the Polytechnic) | ~10,500 | NUST/Wikipedia |

What these numbers say, in one line each:

1. **The demand pool is enormous and under-served.** Over half a million
   young Namibians are outside the labour force entirely. Universities take a
   fraction of school leavers; the rest need an applied, employable,
   venture-capable education.
2. **The TVET system is growing but far from target.** The national system is
   short of its own 45,000 target, which means seats exist for a credible
   private provider, especially one with a different offer.
3. **The money for training already exists.** Employers have paid a VET levy
   since 2014. Employer-sponsored upskilling (section 4.2) is the
   highest-leverage revenue channel for a new institute, because the
   training budget is already being collected.
4. **Entrepreneurship education is a documented national gap.** Research
   keeps finding that EE in Namibia is thin, optional and policy-light at
   tertiary level. Oryx making it compulsory in every programme is a genuine
   differentiator, not a marketing line.

## 3. Positioning: "Stanford on steroids", translated for Windhoek

| Stanford ingredient | Oryx translation |
|---|---|
| World-class teaching core | Practitioner faculty: people who build for a living, teaching small cohorts |
| d.school (design thinking everywhere) | Applied problem-solving as the fourth discipline and the named certificate standard |
| StartX / ecosystem | Every cohort ships micro-ventures; Oryx Ventures (phase 3) funds the best ones |
| Research engine | Not at launch. The AI-native toolchain (section 5) is Oryx's research surface instead |
| Endowment + brand moat | Speed + honesty: launch small, publish everything, win the trust race |

**AI-native means, concretely:**
- Every course runs a GLM-backed study companion (tutoring, practice,
  translation between English, Oshiwambo, Afrikaans, Khoekhoegowab where
  feasible) instead of a help desk that answers in three days.
- Every staff member works with agents for grading prep, course materials,
  and administration. The staff plan (section 6) hires for this.
- The institute publishes its own stack openly (section 5.3), which is both
  pedagogy and marketing.

**What Oryx is not:** not a university replica, not a certificate mill, not a
bootcamp. The certificate language is approved: "Excellence in innovation and
applied problem-solving." Everything the institute sells must be defensible
against that line.

## 4. Go-to-market (gtm-strategy framework)

### 4.1 Segments, in confidence order

| # | Segment | Pain | Oryx answer | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | School leavers without university seats | No applied option that leads to work | Certificate programmes, small cohorts, venture spine | WhatsApp, NBC radio, school career fairs |
| S2 | Employed youth reskilling for the AI era | Skills expiring faster than employers retrain | Evening/short courses, employer-sponsored | LinkedIn, employer direct, levy angle |
| S3 | Employers (private + parastatal) | Levy paid, training return unclear | Funded cohorts, custom short courses, train-the-team | Direct founder-led sales |
| S4 | Micro-entrepreneurs (informal to formal) | Tools and bookkeeping, not theory | Weekend venture courses, AI tools training | Facebook, community radio, SME networks |

Start with S1 + S3 together: S1 fills the first cohort, S3 pays for it.

### 4.2 The levy insight (the unfair advantage)

Every qualifying Namibian employer already pays the VET levy. A new institute
that can register with the NTA and structure employer programmes inside the
grant/rebate rules is not asking employers for new money; it is offering a
better return on money they already spend. The action item is to map the NTA
levy grant rules exactly before the first employer conversation. This is the
single highest-value research task on the critical path.

### 4.3 Messaging pillars

1. **"Looks harmless. Isn't."** (approved) — the sleeper advantage: a small
   institute that out-teaches the big ones.
2. **The tools motto** — AI-native education, the brain as the biggest tool.
3. **Entrepreneurship in everything** — every programme ends with something
   that works and something that sells.
4. **Solve problems quietly, finish them decisively** (approved) — the
   graduate identity. All campaign copy speaks to this person.

### 4.4 Channels (few, done excellently)

- **WhatsApp-first funnel** — already built into the site (wa.me handoff with

- **Founder-led radio** — NBC radio and community stations; Namibia still

- **School outreach circuit** — Khomas Region career fairs, Grade 10–12
  assemblies, one honest "what is a politechnical" talk.
- **Facebook + LinkedIn** — Facebook for S1/S4 parents and learners,
  LinkedIn for S2/S3 employers.
- **The Resources & Insights hub** (section 5.1) as inbound: every article
  answers a real search question Namibian parents and school leavers ask.

### 5.2 `/compare` — honest comparison pages (requested, must exist)

Comparison pages against fellow Namibian institutions. Rules of engagement,
non-negotiable:

1. **Never disparage.** Every comparison table uses publicly verifiable
   facts, cited, and states Oryx's own status honestly (new, small,
   pre-accreditation at launch).
2. **Compare on the axes where Oryx actually differs:** AI-native toolchain,
   compulsory entrepreneurship spine, cohort size, practitioner faculty,
   venture outcomes. Do not compare on history, rankings or facilities.
3. **First pages:** Oryx vs NUST (the polytechnic lineage), Oryx vs a
   government VTC, Oryx vs private colleges generally, Oryx vs going it
   alone (self-taught route).
4. Each page ends in the same honest close: "If you want big, go to them.
   If you want this, apply."

## 6. Staff strategy (the "other staff" plan)

### 6.1 The AI-native staffing ratio

A traditional college of 200 students hires roughly 15–20 admin and support
staff. Oryx's plan: **1 admin + agents.** Admissions triage, scheduling,
records, and first-line communication run on the GLM toolchain with human
review. This is not about cutting people; it is about spending the payroll
on faculty, which is what families are actually buying.

### 6.2 Faculty: practitioner-teachers, two tracks

- **Core faculty (full-time):** small, senior, each one owns a school spine
  and teaches across programmes.
- **Practitioner adjuncts (part-time):** working engineers, developers,
  accountants, tradespeople who teach one evening block. Paid per block.
  This is how a startup institute teaches wide with a small payroll.

### 6.3 Hiring bar (write this into the /people page)

1. Can you do the thing, recently, for real clients or employers?
2. Can you teach it to someone who failed school? (demo lesson required)
3. Do you use the tools? (the AI-native bar; we teach the stack we run)
4. Will you hold the Oryx voice: honest, plain, no theatre?

### 6.4 Key-person risk, stated plainly

Right now the institute is one person and a brand. The staff plan's first
job is to make the institute survivable without any single person (section
8, risk R2): documented admissions process, a second signatory on
communications, and the agent toolchain holding the operational memory.

## 7. The critical path: accreditation and compliance

This is the part that kills startup institutes that skip it.

1. **NTA registration** for vocational programmes; **NQA accreditation** for
   qualifications. Map the exact requirements, timelines and costs in the
   first 30 days (this map does not guess them).
2. Until accredited, Oryx sells **short courses and institute certificates**
   honestly labelled as such, and says so on every page. The comparison
   pages (5.2) state this as a fact, not a footnote.
3. The certificate already exists as a brand artifact ("Certificate of
   Achievement"). Keep its language exactly as approved.
4. Guardrail for all site copy: never imply NQA accreditation, never name
   partner employers that are not signed, never publish a launch date the
   Principal has not confirmed.

### 8.2 Contingency plan (business-continuity framework, startup-sized)

| # | Risk | Impact | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Accreditation delayed beyond two intake cycles | High | Filing milestones slipping | Stay honest-certificates; run short-course revenue; quarterly NTA status reviews |
| R2 | Key-person dependency (Principal) | Critical | Single approval bottleneck | Documented ops, agent-held process memory, second comms signatory |
| R3 | Enrolment under 50% of cohort 1 target | High | Enquiry pipeline < 60 by day 60 | Go/no-go gate at day 90; delay cohort, not quality; convert enquiries to short courses |
| R4 | Funding runway < 6 months | Critical | Cash model drift | Short-course revenue first; employer seats second; premises cost is the lever to cut |
| R5 | Premises unavailable | Medium | Lease search stalls | Hourly workshop rental; evening-only timetables at partner venues |
| R6 | Connectivity/power disruption | Low–medium | Any outage | Offline-first materials (textbook skill outputs are downloadable); mobile-data fallback for agents |
| R7 | Reputational (overpromising) | Critical | Any claim we cannot evidence | The section 7 guardrail list is a publishing gate, enforced before anything ships |

Review cadence: the risk table is re-read at every 30-day roadmap gate. The
founder owns it; the map keeps it honest.
