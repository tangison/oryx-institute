# Oryx Research Proof brief

**Creative authority:** Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. The user selected **Use your judgment** for the page purpose, visual direction, sequence and structure in the Scrollcraft interview on 25 November 2026. The decisions below are authored, not quoted user statements.

## 1. Vibe and references

- **User response:** Use the existing Oryx direction.
- **Authored direction:** Quietly exact, material, editorial, field-led and public.
- **Reference boundary:** The page takes its visual cues from a working proof sheet, field notebook and a restrained print room. It does not copy a named website, become a magazine pastiche or use a generic software interface.

## 2. Scroll sequence

- **User response:** Shape the sequence.
- **Authored sequence:** A visitor first sees research as a real working surface, then understands how a question becomes evidence, enters a held materials act, reaches the forthcoming-publication reveal, reads the notes and ends with one direct research-enquiry route.

## 3. Energy curve

- **User response:** Shape the sequence.
- **Authored curve:** Begin composed and tactile. Tighten into method. Hold at the publication reveal. Release into slow reading. End settled and practical.

## 4. Feeling curve and peak

| Act | Intended feeling | What on screen causes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | Focus | A layered research table, folio and paper edge occupy separate planes while the title remains on a clean sheet. |
| Question | Confidence | A plain explanation of how a defined question becomes a documented answer grounded in evidence. |
| Materials | Attention | One research material row holds at a time against the field-note texture. |
| Publication reveal, the peak | Anticipation | A two-leaf proof sheet opens with the scroll and holds the forthcoming-publication announcement. |
| Notes | Trust | Dated research notes remain readable in normal editorial flow. |
| Reply | Readiness | One consistent enquiry action closes the page after named relationships. |

**Peak:** The publication reveal. A visitor should say: “The next Oryx publication feels like work that is being prepared, not a generic coming-soon promise.”

## 5. Signature move

- **User response:** Use your judgment.
- **Authored signature:** **The proof-sheet fold.** The publication mock-up becomes two paper leaves that separate with scroll progress, then settle around the forthcoming-publication text. It is page-specific, uses the supplied local image and becomes static when motion is reduced.

## 6. Aesthetic range

- **User response:** Use the existing Oryx direction.
- **Authored range:** Premium-minimal with a stronger physical editorial rhythm. The ink, cream and maroon palette remains restrained. There are no counters, fake data, loud effects, baked-in words or synthetic people.

## 7. Structure

- **User response:** Use your judgment.
- **Authored grammar:** **Research proof folio.** It is a sequence of separate paper, field and ledger scenes rather than a continuous world.
- **Navigation:** The existing accessible global masthead remains. The page carries one local reading anchor, not a second navigation system.
- **Hero:** A three-plane research surface: field table, ledger detail and publication sheet. The title sits on its own cream paper ground.
- **Ending:** A reply slip with the same research-enquiry action used at the beginning. The footer remains the final site-wide close.
- **Bans:** No chapter numbers, full-frame opaque overlays, card-grid repetition, scroll prompts, autoplay media, generic magnetic buttons or empty pinned time.

## 8. Assets

- **User response:** Use your judgment.
- **Available assets:** Existing local research imagery: `research-ground-table.jpg`, `research-ledger-detail.jpg`, `24-research-field-notes.jpg`, `17-brass-calipers.jpg` and `research-publication-coming-soon.jpg`.
- **Asset decision:** Use only these existing editorial images. No asset generation is required, no external request is introduced and locked files in `public/brand/` remain untouched.

## Purpose, belief and action

- **Audience:** People and organisations with a defined Namibian market-research question, plus readers assessing Oryx’s public research posture.
- **Belief:** Oryx treats research as a practical, inspectable process and is building a publication from that work.
- **One action:** **Bring a Market R&D problem.** The label stays unchanged wherever the page asks for contact.

## Tell-someone sentence

“It’s the research page where a field notebook opens into the next Oryx publication, then lets you inspect the work behind it.”

## Authored silence

The caliper divider is intentional silence between the publication reveal and the dated notes. It gives the notes room to read and must remain a short material pause rather than a missing section.
